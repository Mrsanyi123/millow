Before we meet,
a few words.

October 2026

Most days go by faster than we can make sense of them. Something good happens at ten in the morning and by dinner it’s gone. Something hard happens, and it sits with you for a week, and no one quite knows. You meant to write it down. You meant to call someone back. You meant to remember.

We’re building Millow for those days, which is to say, for every day.

I. What Millow is

Millow is an AI companion that talks with you about your day. It isn’t a chatbot you open when something breaks, and it isn’t another productivity tool asking for an hour of your attention. It’s closer to a friend with a very good memory: someone who checks in with you in the evening, asks how the thing you were worried about went, and actually wants to know.

You talk, out loud, the way you would to someone who cares. It listens. And slowly, over weeks and months, it comes to understand you: the people in your life, the goals you keep circling back to, and the patterns that are hard to see from the inside.

II. How it works

It begins with a conversation. Each evening, or whenever you like, Millow says hey. Tell it what happened, what you’re proud of, what got under your skin. Speak if you can, type if you can’t. There’s no form to fill in and no right way to do it.

It asks, the way a friend would. Mention a fight with a friend and it won’t move on to the next question. It’ll ask what happened, and it won’t judge you for your side of it.

It does the writing for you. When you say goodnight, Millow turns the conversation into a journal page in your own words. It notes how you were feeling, saves the wins, and keeps track of anything you said you’d do.

It remembers what matters. Millow keeps an understanding of you, not a transcript. So when you mention your sister next month, it already knows her name, and remembers she just started somewhere new.

It shows you how far you’ve come. Over time, those moments add up to something you can see: your wins, your struggles, your progress. On the days it feels like nothing is moving, Millow can show you that it is.

III. It knows your world, too

Your life doesn’t live in one place, so neither does Millow. Connect your calendar, and it knows Thursday is the big presentation, so it asks how it went on Thursday night. Mention your grandmother’s birthday, and with your OK, it’s on your calendar. Say you need to call the dentist, and it’s on your list before you’ve finished the sentence. Connect Google Docs, and it can bring back the notes you wrote when you need them. Gmail, Drive, Notion and more are on their way.

This is where a companion becomes useful. Millow can remind you of the follow-up you promised, help you get ready for tomorrow, and notice when your week is quietly filling up too much, before you do.

IV. Where Millow is going

Today, Millow starts as an evening conversation on your phone. That’s the beginning, not the shape of the whole thing.

We imagine a Millow that’s with you wherever you are: on your laptop at your desk, in your earbuds on a walk home, maybe one day in something you wear. One that checks in at the moments that matter, not only at night: the morning of a big interview, the evening after a long week, the quiet stretch when you haven’t talked to it in a while.

We imagine a Millow that sees the bigger picture. That notices you sleep better after days you go outside, that certain people always leave you lighter, that you’ve mentioned the same dream three times this year and still haven’t started. Not to lecture you, but to hand you back what you already know about yourself.

And we imagine a Millow that ties your life together. Your calendar, your notes, your messages, the other AI tools you use: today they each hold a piece of you, and none of them know how the pieces fit. Millow isn’t meant to be one more app. It’s meant to be the one that understands how all the others fit into your life, and helps you act on it.

Years from now, we want you to be able to ask Millow what you were worried about the spring you turned twenty, and have it remember. A life, kept.

V. What we promise

What you tell Millow is yours. It is never sold, never used to target ads, and never shared without your say. You can see everything it remembers, change any of it, or ask it to forget. It will always ask before it acts on your behalf. And it will never pretend to be a replacement for the people in your life, or for real help when you need it; when something is heavier than a conversation, it will point you toward them.

A companion is only worth having if you can trust it completely, so trust is where we started.

VI. Why we’re building it

Millow started with something small. I noticed I could talk about my day for twenty minutes without trying, but couldn’t make myself write a single line in a diary. So I wondered: what if the talking was enough?

We believe everyone deserves to feel a little more understood, and not by an algorithm guessing what you’ll buy next. We mean something that listens the way a good friend does, and remembers so you don’t have to carry everything alone.

We’re opening Millow to a small group first. If any of this sounds like something you’ve been missing, we would love for you to be one of them.

With warmth,

Millow, by Sanyi Diriba

P.S. It’s alright if today was an ordinary day. Those are worth remembering, too.
